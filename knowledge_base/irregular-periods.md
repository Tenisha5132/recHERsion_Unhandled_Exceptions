Irregular periods are not usually a sign of a problem, but changes in pattern should be discussed with a GP if persistent. The average menstrual cycle is around 28 days, but can be shorter or longer. Periods may be considered irregular if the gap between them is less than 21 days or more than 35 days. 

Irregular periods are more common when they first start during puberty and when nearing menopause. Common causes include puberty, menopause transition, pregnancy (a missed period can be an early sign), hormonal contraception, weight changes, stress, anxiety, or exercising too much. Sometimes conditions like polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS, previously called polycystic ovary syndrome/PCOS) or thyroid problems can cause irregular periods. 

It can be normal for cycles to be irregular for the first 1-2 years after periods start. Keeping a diary or app of periods can help track patterns. See a GP if periods are irregular and persistent, last longer than 7 days, are accompanied by other symptoms like weight gain, tiredness, excess hair growth, or oily/dry skin, if there is bleeding between periods, or if struggling to get pregnant. Getting pregnant may be more difficult with irregular periods as ovulation may not happen regularly - having sex every 2-3 days throughout the cycle can help when trying to conceive.
