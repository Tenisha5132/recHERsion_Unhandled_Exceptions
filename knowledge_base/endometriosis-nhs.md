Endometriosis is where cells similar to those in the lining of the womb grow in other parts of the body. It can have a big impact on life, but treatments can help. Patches can grow around the womb, ovaries, fallopian tubes, pelvis, and sometimes elsewhere like bladder, bowel or chest. When they break down and bleed, they can't leave the body causing symptoms. 

Symptoms may include severe period pain that stops normal activities, heavy periods needing to change pads/tampons every 1-2 hours, pelvic/lower back pain, pain when pooing or peeing, pain during or after sex, extreme tiredness, pain/bleeding elsewhere, difficulty getting pregnant, low mood or anxiety. 

Diagnosis can take time as symptoms overlap with other conditions; tests may include exam, ultrasound, MRI, or laparoscopy (which can also allow removal). Treatment isn't curative - options include painkillers, hormonal treatments, surgery to remove endometriosis tissue/cysts or in severe cases hysterectomy/oophorectomy, and support for pain, fertility and mental health.
