Heavy periods, also called menorrhagia, can affect anyone who has periods and may be normal for some people. They become a concern when they interfere with daily life. Signs that bleeding may be heavy include needing to change a pad or tampon every one to two hours, using two types of period products together, periods lasting more than seven days, passing blood clots larger than about the size of a 10p coin, bleeding through clothes or bedding, avoiding activities, or feeling tired or short of breath due to blood loss. 

Periods can be heavier at certain times - when they first start, after pregnancy, or as someone approaches menopause. Possible causes include conditions affecting the womb or ovaries such as polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS/PCOS), fibroids, endometriosis, adenomyosis, or pelvic inflammatory disease. Some people bleed more easily due to conditions like Von Willebrand disease, certain medicines (including anticoagulants), or chemotherapy. Rarely, heavy periods can be a sign of womb cancer. 

Treatment isn't always needed, but options may include hormonal contraception, medicines to reduce bleeding such as tranexamic acid, anti-inflammatory painkillers, or specialist treatments if simpler measures don't help. Anyone with heavy periods affecting daily life, persistent heavy bleeding, severe pain, bleeding between periods or after sex, or other concerning symptoms should speak to a GP.
