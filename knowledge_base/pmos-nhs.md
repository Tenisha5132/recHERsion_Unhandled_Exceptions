Polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS) is a hormone condition that can affect hair growth, periods, fertility and mood. PMOS used to be called polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). There is no cure, but treatments and lifestyle changes can help. 

Symptoms usually start in girls and young women at puberty and continue until menopause; some symptoms may change after menopause. Main symptoms include irregular periods or long gaps between periods, excessive hair growth or hair loss, weight gain or difficulty losing weight, difficulty getting pregnant, mental health problems (depression/anxiety), oily skin and acne, feeling very tired, and thick dark patches of skin (acanthosis nigricans). Symptoms can be mild, severe, or absent. 

Tests may include blood tests to check hormone levels and insulin resistance, and an ultrasound scan to look at the ovaries (ultrasound only done if over 18). Treatment can include hormonal contraception to regulate periods, medicines to help fertility or reduce hair growth, metformin, acne treatments, weight management, or laparoscopic ovarian drilling in some fertility cases. Lifestyle measures like healthy diet, regular exercise, weight loss if overweight, and support for mental health can help. Complications can include type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and womb cancer - regular check-ups can help reduce risk.
