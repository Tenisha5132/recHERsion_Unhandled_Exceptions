An underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) is when the thyroid gland doesn't make enough hormones. Symptoms develop slowly and can include tiredness, feeling cold, weight gain, constipation, difficulty concentrating, low mood/depression, dry skin/hair/hair loss, hoarse voice, and irregular or heavy periods. 

It is commonly caused by Hashimoto's disease (autoimmune). Diagnosis is by blood tests checking TSH and thyroxine. Treatment is usually lifelong levothyroxine tablets to replace thyroid hormone, with regular blood tests to adjust dose. If untreated or under-treated, risks include heart problems and bone problems; very rarely myxoedema coma can occur. If pregnant or planning pregnancy, thyroid treatment needs monitoring as it can affect pregnancy outcomes.
